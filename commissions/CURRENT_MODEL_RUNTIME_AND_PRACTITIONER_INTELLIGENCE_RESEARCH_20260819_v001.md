Produce decision-grade intelligence, matched Obzio evaluation designs and exact purchase options. Do not select a winner from reputation, public benchmark rank, a remembered practitioner, a single release or one provider's marketing.

## 5. Practitioner-intelligence acceptance

Prioritise sources by currentness:

- **Tier A:** published in the last 0–7 days;
- **Tier B:** published 8–30 days ago and still version-current;
- **Tier C:** older only where it supplies durable method, reproducible artefacts, historical comparison or a failure not otherwise available.

A high-signal practitioner should demonstrate some combination of:

- real local or controlled deployment;
- multiple-model comparative operation;
- exact model, quantisation, runtime and hardware detail;
- coding-agent or long-horizon workflow use;
- repository, script, trace, configuration, evaluation or reproducible demonstration;
- cost, latency, memory and throughput reporting with units;
- failure disclosure and changed method;
- evidence of sustained use rather than a launch-day test; and
- relevance to Obzio's workloads and operating constraints.

Follower count, popularity, a polished video and repeated vendor claims are not evidence of competence.

For every practitioner/source, create or update a source card with:

`identity | exact locator | publication date | version/date of system used | demonstrated experience | proposition supported | evidence method | reproducibility | incentives/conflicts | contradictions | rights/sensitivity | validity/recheck | Obzio object changed | lifecycle`.

Do not guess `Sina` or `Thomas/TMS`. First recover their exact founder-supplied locator or context. If unresolved, preserve that status and continue independent discovery.

No practitioner outreach is authorised.

## 8. Hardware and local-runtime decision

Return lean, recommended and premium owned-compute options only after determining:

- target models and quantisations;
- memory/VRAM requirements;
- expected context and concurrent-agent workloads;
- power, thermal, noise and physical constraints;
- runtime/driver support;
- upgrade path;
- local-versus-hosted split;
- data/security posture;
- availability, warranty and total cost; and

A purchase packet must state:

- exact item/class and purpose;
- decision changed;
- evidence and alternatives;
- price and recurring cost current at decision time;
- compatibility and availability;
- expected workload coverage;
- risks and reversal path;
- what can proceed without purchase; and
- the smallest exact founder decision required.
